# Changelog

## Unreleased

FoliageFX began as a one-file prototype (`Custom/Scripts/VampixCollections/FoliageFX/FoliageFX.cs`, a plugin class with a `FallingLeaves` block at the end, and its shader in `foliagefx.assetbundle`). It fixed shadowless leaves: tree and plant CustomUnityAssets that ship their leaves on the Standard shader in Fade or Transparent mode render them in the Transparent queue, which Unity's built-in pipeline never shadows, so the leaves glowed in shade; it assigned copies of those materials on an alpha-tested shader and put the originals back on disable or remove. It then grew a material editor (an atom chooser and a material chooser in VaM's stock right column, a mode per material, Auto, Convert or Keep original), recolouring (tint, hue, saturation, brightness), a wind sway, and falling leaves cut from the tree's own texture. On the way it hit VaM's compiler bug: the runtime compiler crashes on `foreach` over `Dictionary.Values`, so the prototype kept its state in Lists iterated by index. That crash is why the sandbox lint exists here too: `tools/lint.ps1` refuses `foreach` over any `.Keys` or `.Values`, and a `JSONClass` is iterated as `KeyValuePair<string, JSONNode>`.

The prototype is reworked into Live Skin's layout and tooling. The one file is split by role into `Types/Core`, `Types/Scene`, `Types/Effects` and `Types/Ui`, so each thing the plugin makes has one owner with the restore it owes, and the effects stop reaching into each other: the plugin class's scan and precedence check became `TreeRegistry`, its per-atom state `Tree`, its per-material entry `LeafMaterial` (the renderer slots and the restore), its detection `LeafRules`, its per-material settings `LeafLook` kept by `TreeLooks`, its bundle loading `LeafShader`, the conversion `ShadowFix`, the sway `Wind`, and the `FallingLeaves` block `LeafSprites`, `LeafEmitter` and the `FallingLeaves` feature; `Lifecycle` runs them in one documented order, and the facade keeps VaM's lifecycle and the scene JSON. Every constant moved into `FoliageFXConfig`. The namespace, the nested types and the extra public types went (one public type, `FoliageFX`, no namespaces), and so did the version number in code, which the packager now stamps. The tooling is Live Skin's, adapted: `tools/setup.ps1`, the git hooks (lint and compile on commit, the bundle rebuilt when `shader/` changes, the var built and compiled on push, `build.json` after every commit, checkout and merge), `tools/pack.ps1` with the Roslyn comment stripper, `tools/build-info.ps1`, and CI. The UI kit is Live Skin's too (`UiHost`, `UiKit`, `UiShell`, `FeatureView`, `ConfirmBox`, `ReportRow`, `SmoothScroll`), with two additions: `UiShell.OnApplied`, run after each Apply, and `RackRow.SetBadge`, a row's amber badge with its tooltip.

The panel is Live Skin's shell with three categories: Overview ("Status and Trees"), Trees ("Fix and Recolour") and Effects ("Shadows, Wind, Leaves"). Trees has a live rack: one row per CustomUnityAsset, made and hidden at runtime as atoms come and go (`TreesPanel` keeps a pool of `FeatureView`s keyed by uid, added through `shell.AddFeature` with the id `tree:<uid>` and built with `kit.RackRow` into the rack card), each with `Tree.State()` and a switch, a per-tree proxy storable that is never registered. A tree can now be switched off on its own, which the prototype could not do. The prototype's right column became the tree editor, one shared editor in `TreesPanel`'s own bag shown while a tree is selected: the tree's `Enabled` and status, a Materials card (Material, a live line with the material's shader, queue and the verdict's reason, the falling leaves line, Mode), Look, Leaves, then Reset Material, Copy Look to Tree, Copy to Same Material Everywhere, and a new Reset to Defaults for the whole tree. Its controls are `ParamSet` entries (feature `tree`, Volatile and Private): the selected look is pushed into them without callbacks, and a change writes the look, marks it customised, and re-applies that one tree (`Lifecycle.Touch`). Effects is a fixed rack, Shadow Fix, Wind and Falling Leaves, each with an inspector: a guide, cards, a hint under every control whose label does not say what it does, a story box where there is something the hints do not say, and its cost with Technical Info. The Overview carries the identity card, What's New, the Diagnostics card (the status line, Rescan, Technical Info, Show Hints, Diagnose) and a dashboard of every tree and every effect.

A FoliageFX on the atom itself takes precedence. A FoliageFX on a CustomUnityAsset owns that tree; a scene-wide one, on any other atom or as a session plugin, looks after every CustomUnityAsset without one of its own and lists the others as handled elsewhere: the row reads "own FoliageFX", carries the amber badge ("This atom has its own FoliageFX, which takes precedence.") and has no switch. Another instance is recognised by a storable id ending in `_FoliageFX` (VaM names a plugin's storable `plugin#N_<class>`), checked on every scan, so a tree whose owner changes is released and re-applied within one scan, and removing the atom's own instance hands the tree back. A material that is already one of ours (its name ends in ` (FoliageFX)`, or it is on the leaf shader) is another instance's copy and is never converted again ("other FoliageFX").

Scene persistence is under keys, with the trees in their own node. The prototype registered its settings with VaM under their display names ("Wind strength", "Falling leaf size (m)") and kept the customised materials in a `materials` node, the mode as its display word and the tint as three fields. The plugin now writes one block, `FoliageFX: {FoliageFXSchema, version, params, trees}`: every setting under a stable `<feature>.<param>` key (`wind.direction_deg`, `falling.size_mm`, the size now in millimetres), registered with VaM for Timeline and triggers but not stored by it, and the `trees` node keyed by atom uid, or `self` for the atom the plugin sits on, each tree `{enabled, materials: {<name>: look}}` with only the customised looks and the trees switched off. A look's keys are snake case and frozen (`mode`, `tint`, `hue`, `saturation`, `brightness`, `cutoff`, `smoothness`, `translucency`, `double_sided`, `falling`), the mode a chooser id (`auto`, `convert`, `keep`), the tint "h,s,v" and every float in the invariant culture. A duplicate material name within a tree gets " #2". A rename moves the tree's key (`onAtomUIDRenameHandlers`, `TreeLooks.Rename`), and a restore whose node carries nothing, VaM's shape for an atom's Reset, is ignored rather than read as every value at its default. The schema starts at 1.

The leaf shader is renamed and its bundle is built by script. `VampixCollections/FoliageFX Leaves (Standard Lit)` in `foliagefx.assetbundle`, built by a Unity editor script run by hand (`Source/FoliageBuild.cs.txt`), is now `Vampix Collections/FoliageFX/Leaves` in `shader/Leaves.shader`, and the bundle is `FoliageFX.assetbundle`, built by `shader/Editor_BuildFoliageFXBundle.cs` through `tools/bundle.ps1` (Unity 2018.1.9f1 in batch mode) and recorded in `shader/bundle.sources`, so the pre-commit hook rebuilds it when `shader/` changes and the packager and CI refuse a stale one. The property names the runtime writes are frozen: `_Color _MainTex _Cutoff _BumpMap _BumpScale _Glossiness _Metallic _HueShift _Saturation _Brightness _Translucency _TranslucencyDistortion _TranslucencyPower _SoftEdges _MipCoverage _WindStrength _WindSpeed _WindDirection _Cull`, with the `_NORMALMAP` keyword, the AlphaTest queue, and a ShadowCaster pass with AlphaToMask off.

Falling leaves take their leaf shapes from the texture in a class of their own. `LeafSprites` holds the prototype's extraction: the leaf texture read at most 1024 on a side, the connected opaque regions not cut by its border, between a minimum and a maximum share of its area, not long twigs, reasonably solid and not much darker than the typical shape (buds, berries and bark stay out), the mask thinned up to two texels to cut leaves from twigs, the best sixteen packed into a 512 atlas of 4 x 4 tiles; a texture with fewer than three shapes gives rounded pieces of itself instead. It depends only on UnityEngine and `FoliageFXConfig`, so it compiles outside VaM. `LeafEmitter` is one material's falling and resting particle systems. `FallingLeaves` runs one extraction per source texture, shared by every material that uses it, as a coroutine that yields between steps, dropped and retried when it has not answered in 15 s, its results destroyed on release and when the texture is gone; one emitter per converted material whose look has Drop Falling Leaves on, the rate per tree shared among them, with a leaf material on the leaf shader that takes the look's colours, is double-sided and does not sway. The tree editor's line says what a material drops ("Falling leaves: 15 leaf shapes from the texture.").

A review pass after the rework. Two instances no longer fight over a material: a release puts the source back only into the slots that still show this instance's copy (`LeafMaterial.Restore`), and another instance's copy is read again only once that instance has given the slot back (`TreeRegistry.Stale`), so a scene-wide FoliageFX beside a session one leaves the other's work in place instead of re-reading the tree every two seconds and wiping its copies; two scene-wide instances are still not a supported setup, and the first to convert a material keeps it. The looks of an atom that leaves the scene are forgotten with its tree (`TreeLooks.Forget`), so a session plugin does not carry one scene's looks into the next and a new atom reusing a deleted uid starts clean. A tree keeps its ground as an offset from its loader, so falling leaves made after the atom was moved land at its new foot. `ShadowFix.Switch` puts the source back before it destroys the old copy, so a `MakeCopy` that throws never leaves a renderer with a destroyed material, and a tree's release destroys a copy an effect's hook missed. A leaf-shape job that has not answered in fifteen seconds is retried on the scan tick (`FallingLeaves.Tick`) rather than on that tree's next apply, and a tree's leaf rate is shared only among the materials that have shapes to drop. All Atoms off on an instance that does not sit on a CustomUnityAsset no longer tracks nothing. The Trees page builds its row text only while the rack or the dashboard is shown, and once per tick: the UI tick runs the shell's Apply, whose `OnApplied` syncs the page, and the Overview's refresh after it. `Lifecycle` lost its late-attach paths (the facade wires the effects before `Attach`) and the unused `Held`, `MsLast`, `MsAverage` and `MsWorst`; the timing thresholds moved into `FoliageFXConfig`. Wording: a tree whose leaves wait for a fix that is not running reads "not converted" ("off" is its own switch), the status counts leaf materials "converted", the release reason says Enabled off, the verdicts no longer repeat the queue the line above them shows, and the Brightness, Translucency and Size hints and the Shadow Fix story say what the controls do rather than how. `tools/lint.ps1` also refuses namespaces, `using static` and `foreach` over a Dictionary's pairs, and its generic-method rule no longer needs a T-named type parameter.
